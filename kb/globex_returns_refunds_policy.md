Globex Returns and Refunds Policy 

— Seller & Buyer Reference
FICTIONAL DOCUMENT. Globex is an invented online marketplace. This policy, the company, and every detail below were written for a portfolio demonstration. Nothing here describes any real company's policy.

Document type: Internal policy reference Owner: Trust & Payments Version: 4.2 Last updated: 2026-06-15 Applies to: US, UK, DE marketplaces


1. Standard return window
Buyers may request a return within 30 days of delivery for items covered by Globex Buyer Protection. Sellers may offer a longer window (60 or 90 days) at their discretion; the seller-set window takes precedence when it is more generous than the standard 30 days.

Items marked "No returns accepted" remain covered by Globex Buyer Protection when the item arrives damaged, is not as described, or never arrives. Seller return preferences do not override Buyer Protection.
Categories excluded from the standard window
Vehicles, real estate, and heavy machinery (separate dispute process)
Digital goods and downloadable content
Perishable items and custom-made goods, unless not as described

3. Refund timing — standard process
Under the standard process, a seller has 2 business days after the returned item is delivered back to them to issue a refund. If the seller has not refunded within that window, the buyer may ask Globex to step in, and Globex will issue the refund and seek reimbursement from the seller.

The full standard sequence is:

Buyer opens a return request
Seller accepts and provides a return shipping label
Buyer ships the item back
Carrier delivers the item to the seller
Seller inspects and issues refund within 2 business days
Refund settles to the buyer's original payment method in 3–5 business days

End to end, a buyer typically waits 8 to 14 days from opening the request to seeing money back.
4. Project Halo — refund at carrier scan
Effective 2026-06-15, Globex began a phased rollout of Project Halo, which changes step 4 above for eligible buyers.

For buyers in the Project Halo cohort, the refund is issued at the moment the carrier records the first acceptance scan of the return parcel — not when the item arrives back with the seller. This removes 5 to 9 days of waiting for the buyer.
Buyer eligibility
A buyer is placed in the cohort when all of the following hold:

Account in good standing for at least 12 months
No more than one return dispute resolved against them in the prior 12 months
Return value at or below the category threshold (USD 200 for most categories)
Return is being shipped with a Globex-provided tracked label
What changes for sellers
The seller is not paid ahead of the return arriving, and the seller's payout is debited at the point of carrier scan rather than at the point of delivery. This means:

Sellers see the refund deduction on their payout before the item is physically back in their possession
If the returned parcel is lost in transit, Globex — not the seller — absorbs the loss, provided the Globex-issued label was used
If the returned item arrives damaged, missing parts, or is not the item that was sent, the seller must open a Return Discrepancy claim within 5 business days of delivery
Return Discrepancy claims
Sellers who receive a materially different item than what they sold may claim reimbursement. Required evidence: photographs of the received item and packaging, the original listing, and the return tracking number. Claims are decided within 7 business days. Approved claims are reimbursed to the seller's payout account in full.

Sellers are not notified in advance that a given buyer is in the Project Halo cohort. Cohort membership is not displayed on the order detail page in the current release.

6. Escalation
Buyers may ask Globex to step in 3 business days after a return request goes unanswered. Sellers may appeal a decision within 30 days. Project Halo cases follow the same escalation path, with the Return Discrepancy claim as the seller's primary remedy.

For support representatives: cases where a seller reports being debited before receiving the item should be tagged Returns & Refunds at L1, and the seller should be directed to the Return Discrepancy process rather than the standard dispute flow.
